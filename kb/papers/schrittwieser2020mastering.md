# Mastering Atari, Go, Chess and Shogi by Planning with a Learned Model

## TL;DR

MuZero learns a latent model that predicts only reward, value and policy (no observation
reconstruction) and runs AlphaZero-style MCTS inside it, with no rules or simulator at planning
time. It matches AlphaZero in chess, shogi and Go and sets an Atari state of the art (2041% median
HNS in 20B frames); a sample-efficient Reanalyze variant gets 731% median in 200M frames.

## Problem

Lookahead search (chess, Go) needs the rules or an accurate simulator; model-free RL (the state of
the art in Atari) lacks precise lookahead (Sec. 1, p. 1). Earlier model-based RL models the
observation stream or a reconstructable latent state, which wastes capacity on irrelevant detail and
lagged well-tuned model-free methods in Atari (Sec. 2, p. 2). The paper wants planning with a model
that predicts only what planning needs.

## Method

- The model has three functions (Fig. 1A, p. 3; Sec. 3, p. 4): representation `h` maps the past
  observations to a hidden state s0; dynamics `g` maps (s^(k-1), a^k) to (r^k, s^k), deterministic;
  prediction `f` maps s^k to policy p^k and value v^k. The hidden state has no imposed semantics
  and no reconstruction constraint (Sec. 1, p. 2).
- Acting (Fig. 1B, p. 3): MCTS from s0 inside the learned model; the action is sampled from the root
  visit-count distribution. Training (Fig. 1C): sample a trajectory from the replay buffer, unroll the
  model K=5 steps with the real actions, and train end to end by BPTT.
- Loss (Eq. 1, p. 4): summed over k=0..K, reward loss on observed rewards, value loss on n-step
  bootstrapped targets z (search value bootstrapped n steps ahead; final outcome in board games),
  policy loss towards the MCTS visit distribution, plus L2. Cross-entropy on categorical
  reward/value heads is used in Atari (footnote 1, p. 4; App. F, p. 14).
- Search (App. B, p. 12, Eq. 2-5): pUCT with c1=1.25, c2=19652; one dynamics and one prediction call
  per simulation; Q values min-max normalised over the tree so unbounded values work; n-step backup
  with discount.
- Versus AlphaZero (App. A, p. 11): no simulator for transitions, no legal-action masking inside the
  tree (masked only at the root), no special terminal handling (terminal states are absorbing).
- Atari input (App. E, p. 13): last 32 RGB frames at 96x96 plus the last 32 actions; 18 actions.
  Representation is strided convs and residual blocks down to 6x6x256; dynamics runs at 6x6
  (App. F, p. 14).
- Reanalyze (App. H, p. 15): re-run MCTS on stored trajectories with current parameters for fresh
  policy targets (80% of updates), a target network for value, 2.0 samples per state, n=5.

## Assumptions

- A1: The environment is a single- or two-player MDP with discrete actions (Sec. 3, p. 3; App. E, p. 13).
- A2: Dynamics are modelled deterministically; stochastic transitions are left for future work (Sec. 3, p. 4).
- A3: Training experience is generated by the latest network's own MCTS actors, with environment interaction (Sec. 4, p. 4; App. D, p. 13).
- A4: A reward signal and an episodic return are available (Eq. 1, p. 4).
- A5: Atari results use 50 simulations per move and an action repeat of 4 (Sec. 4, p. 4, Fig. 2, p. 5).
- A6: Atari input is a stack of 32 past frames and 32 past actions (App. E, p. 13).

## Claims

- C1: MuZero matches AlphaZero's strength in chess, shogi and Go without knowing the rules, and slightly exceeds AlphaZero in Go [A1, A2, A3] (Fig. 2, p. 5, Sec. 4, p. 5).
- C2: On all 57 Atari games MuZero reaches 2041.1% median and 4999.2% mean human-normalized score, above R2D2 (1920.6%, 4024.9%) and Ape-X, using 20.0B frames, 12 hours and 1M training steps [A3, A5, A6] (Table 1, p. 6).
- C3: MuZero beats R2D2 in 42 of 57 games and the prior model-based best, SimPLe, in all games it reported [A3, A5] (Sec. 4, p. 5, Table S1, p. 17).
- C4: MuZero Reanalyze with 200M frames reaches 731% median and 2168.9% mean, against 192% (IMPALA), 231% (Rainbow), 431% (LASER) median [A3, A5] (Table 1, p. 6, App. H, p. 15).
- C5: In Go, search with the learned model scales as well as with the real simulator, up to two orders of magnitude longer than the search used in training (about 0.1 s) [A1, A2] (Fig. 3A, p. 7).
- C6: In Atari, gains from more simulations are small: score improves up to about 100 simulations per move and then stays flat or drops slightly, the authors suggesting greater model inaccuracy than in Go [A3, A5] (Fig. 3B, p. 7).
- C7: In Atari, acting by the raw policy network alone (one simulation) also performs well, so the trained policy internalises much of the benefit of search; the policy-improvement gap from search persists throughout training in Ms. Pac-Man [A3, A5] (Sec. 4, p. 6, Fig. S3, p. 20).
- C8: In Ms. Pac-Man a model-free Q-learning objective in the same architecture (no search) matches R2D2's final score but learns more slowly and converges far lower than MuZero's search-based training [A3, A5] (Fig. 3C, p. 7, Sec. 4, p. 6).
- C9: Training MuZero in Ms. Pac-Man with only 6 simulations per move, fewer than its 8 actions, still learns effectively, and more training simulations learn faster, with 5 simulations clearly worse [A3, A5] (Fig. 3D, p. 7, Sec. 4, p. 6).
- C10: Per-game results vary a lot across 10 training runs (Gravitar and Qbert wide, Breakout and Ms. Pac-Man tight) [A3, A5] (Fig. S1, p. 16).
- C11: With no-op starts, MuZero is best in 37 of 57 games and, from human start positions, in 46 of 57 games against Ape-X (6 for humans); it scores below random on Skiing and Solaris and 0 on Montezuma's Revenge and Pitfall with no-op starts [A3, A5] (Table S1, p. 17, Table S2, p. 18).
- C12: Training resources: Atari used 8 TPUs for training and 32 for self-play per game; board games used 16 and 1000 TPUs [A3] (App. G, p. 15).

## Implementation notes

- Trajectories are stored as sequences of length 200; Atari keeps the most recent 125k sequences, sampled with prioritized replay (alpha=beta=1) (source: App. D, p. 13; App. G, p. 15).
- Network is updated every 1000 steps for data generation (source: App. D, p. 13).
- Atari action sampling uses visit counts with temperature 1 for 500k steps, 0.5 for 250k, 0.25 after (source: App. D, Eq. 6, p. 13).
- Discount 0.997; invertible value transform h(x) = sign(x)(sqrt(|x|+1)-1+0.001x); 601-bin categorical support (source: App. C, p. 13; App. F, p. 14).
- Unroll K=5, bootstrap n=10 (Atari), loss scaled by 1/K, gradient into dynamics halved, hidden state min-max scaled to [0,1] (source: App. G, p. 15).
- Batch size 1024 in Atari, 2048 in board games; 1M training steps (source: Sec. 4, p. 4).
- Dirichlet root noise values for Atari are not given in the text; the paper points to the supplementary pseudocode, which was not read (source: App. C, p. 13).
- Reward and value heads in the board games are not given a discount (source: footnote 3, p. 12).

## Limitations / open questions

- Dynamics are deterministic; stochastic environments are left for future work (Sec. 3, p. 4). Imperfect-information games are not addressed (footnote 2, p. 4).
- Search helps little in Atari relative to Go (C6, C7); the authors link this to model inaccuracy.
- Open question: the hidden state is never checked against observations, so how far a rollout is trustworthy for visual dodging is not measured; only score is reported.
- Open question: the scores use a heavy actor-learner setup (billions of frames, TPUs, C2, C12); the paper gives no low-compute figure except Reanalyze at 200M frames.
- Open question (reader's own): with exact emulator savestates, search could step the emulator itself, as AlphaZero does with a real simulator (the Go comparison in C5); the learned model would then buy only speed or a latent, and only if it is cheaper than an emulator step. In Atari the paper tests only the learned model.
- Open question (reader's own): the dynamics function is trained on the actions actually taken (Fig. 1C, p. 3) and observed rewards (Eq. 1, p. 4), so it needs action- and reward-labelled trajectories and cannot be trained from unlabeled video.
- Open question: how would a behaviour-cloned prior be used? The paper only uses its own search-improved policy as target; it does not test a supervised prior.
- Not read: the supplementary pseudocode (an ancillary file on arXiv).
