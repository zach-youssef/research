# Autonomous Cross-Domain Knowledge Transfer in Lifelong Policy Gradient Reinforcement Learning

<!-- Read from the IJCAI open-access PDF (7 PDF pages, 6 of text; p. 7 is references).
The proofs are in an online appendix on the authors' websites, which was not fetched. -->

## TL;DR

Extends PG-ELLA across task domains: policy parameters are Psi^(g) L s (shared basis L, per-domain
projection Psi^(g), sparse per-task s), updated online from one gradient step per task. On control
domains it beats PG-ELLA and PG in DCP and HC, matches PG-ELLA in CP, and speeds learning in a held-out domain (HC).

## Problem

Lifelong RL work had stayed inside one task domain (one state/action space), while cross-domain
transfer methods need a hand-coded inter-task mapping or learn pairwise mappings at a cost that
does not scale beyond a few tasks (Sec. 1, Sec. 3, pp. 1-3). The paper wants an online method
that learns many tasks consecutively, across several domains, and shares knowledge between all
of them without a prior inter-task mapping (Sec. 4, p. 3).

## Method

- Setting: tasks Z(1)..Z(Tmax) arrive one after another, each an MDP with its own state and
  action space; they are partitioned into groups G(1)..G(Gmax), all tasks of a group sharing
  state and action spaces. In the experiments a group is a task domain (Sec. 4, p. 3). Task order
  and interleaving are not controlled by the agent; it does not know Tmax, G, or inter-task
  mappings (Sec. 4, p. 3).
- Parameterisation: a task t in group g has policy parameters theta(t) in R^d(t), where d(t) is
  the state dimension. theta(t) = Psi^(g) L s(t), with L in R^(d x k) shared by all tasks of all
  domains, Psi^(g) in R^(d(t) x d) a per-group projection, s(t) in R^k a sparse per-task vector.
  B^(g) = Psi^(g) L is the group's basis (Sec. 5, Sec. 5.1, Fig. 1, p. 3).
- Objective: the policy-gradient lower bound J_L is written as a KL term (Sec. 2, p. 2); the
  multi-task objective sums over groups and tasks the negative lower bound plus mu1 ||s||_1,
  mu2 ||Psi||_F^2 and mu3 ||L||_F^2 (Eq. 2-3, p. 4).
- Online approximation (as in PG-ELLA, Sec. 5.2, p. 4): replace the dependence on all
  trajectories by a second-order Taylor expansion around alpha(t), the single-task optimum,
  approximated by one natural-gradient step alpha = theta + eta I^-1 grad J (I the Fisher
  matrix). Eq. 5 then minimises ||alpha(t) - Psi L s(t)||^2 weighted by the Hessian Gamma(t), plus
  the three regularisers (Eq. 5, p. 4).
- Update on seeing a task t in group g: gradient steps on L (Eq. 6) and Psi^(g) (Eq. 7) with
  rates eta_L, eta_Psi, then s(t) by a Lasso encoding alpha(t) in the basis Psi^(g) L. s may be
  recomputed less often, letting the policy change as L and Psi move (Sec. 5.2, p. 5).
- Theory: Props. 1-5 (Sec. 6, p. 5); proofs are sketched in the paper, details are in an online
  appendix.
- Experiments: see Claims; setup in Sec. 7, p. 6.

## Assumptions

- A1: All tasks in a group share one state and one action space, and are generated by varying latent parameters (Sec. 4, p. 3).
- A2: Each task's policy parameters are a sparse linear combination of latent components shared across the group (Sec. 5, p. 3).
- A3: The learner does not know Tmax, the task order or distribution, G, or any inter-task mapping a priori (Sec. 4, p. 3).
- A4: The policy has d(t) parameters, the state dimension (Sec. 4, p. 3). Reader's inference: that fits only a linear policy with a single-output mean. It is inconsistent with HC, which has two controls (collective and differential rotor thrust, Sec. 7, p. 6), so the paper's d(t) is probably loose.
- A5: Rewards are the negative Euclidean distance to the goal state minus the action norm (Sec. 7, p. 6). Only HC is stated to be linearised; the dynamics class of the other domains is not given (reader's inference that they are simple low-dimensional systems).
- A6: The learner knows which group each arriving task belongs to (reader's inference, Sec. 5, p. 3).

## Claims

- C1: A new cross-domain lifelong RL method (the authors call it the first, pp. 1, 6): policy parameters theta(t) = Psi^(g) L s(t) with a shared L, per-group Psi^(g) and sparse per-task s(t), learned online [A1, A2] (Sec. 5.1, p. 3).
- C2: PG-ELLA is a special case of the method, when learning is limited to tasks of one domain [A1] (Sec. 3, p. 2).
- C3: The Taylor approximation removes the objective's dependence on all earlier trajectories, so the problem suits online learning [A2] (Sec. 5.2, Eq. 5, p. 4).
- C4: Adding a task to a group changes its projection Psi^(g) by O(1/|G(g)|) (Prop. 1), and changes L by O(sum over groups of 1/|G(g)|) (Prop. 4); the authors frame this as the approach becoming more stable as tasks and groups grow [A1, A2] (Sec. 6, p. 5).
- C5: The surrogate losses for Psi^(g) and for L converge almost surely, and to the actual loss's limit (Props. 2 and 5); the distance of Psi to the actual loss's stationary points goes to 0 a.s. as |G(g)| grows (Prop. 3) [A1, A2] (Sec. 6, p. 5).
- C6: Over interleaved training of tasks from six domains, the method learns faster than PG-ELLA and plain PG in helicopter and double cart-pole, and matches PG-ELLA in cart-pole [A3, A4, A5, A6] (Fig. 2a-c, Sec. 7, p. 6).
- C7: Transfer raises initial performance over PG in every domain except helicopter according to the text; Fig. 2d shows bars for DCP, SM, DM, CP and Bike only [A3, A4, A5, A6] (Fig. 2d, Sec. 7, p. 6).
- C8: In a new domain (helicopter, the most complex), starting from an L trained on all other domains, learning is faster than PG-ELLA and PG from scratch, averaged over ten trials on all three HC tasks [A3, A4, A5, A6] (Fig. 3, Sec. 7, p. 6).

## Implementation notes

- Domains: simple mass (SM), double mass (DM), cart-pole (CP), double cart-pole (DCP), bicycle (Bike), helicopter (HC, linearised CH-47 tandem rotor at 40 knots); 3 tasks each by varying system parameters, 18 total (source: Sec. 7, p. 6).
- Reward -sqrt((x-x_goal)^T (x-x_goal)) - sqrt(a^T a); each task trained on 100 sampled trajectories of length 50 (source: Sec. 7, p. 6).
- Each interleaved run draws a task at random with replacement until all were seen at least once; about 60 rounds per trial, results averaged over about 94 trials per domain (source: Sec. 7, p. 6).
- Base learner Natural Actor Critic; all mu = e^-5; learning rates and latent dimensions (d, k) by cross-validation on a few tasks; the chosen d and k, and the policy's noise model, are not given (source: Sec. 7, p. 6).
- The state dimensions per domain are not tabulated; they follow from the system descriptions for SM (2), DM (4), CP (4) and DCP (6), but not for Bike or HC (source: assumption).
- The authors say the implementation is on their websites; no URL is given in the paper (source: Sec. 5.2, p. 5).
- Hessian Gamma(t) for a linear-Gaussian policy has a closed form; the paper does not say which policy class is used (source: assumption).

## Limitations / open questions

- The paper's policies have as many parameters as the state has dimensions (A4), so the method is shown only for small controllers (linear by inference, A4) on low-dimensional dynamical systems (Sec. 4, Sec. 7, pp. 3, 6).
- No wall-clock or scaling measurements are reported, despite the framing as computationally efficient; only 6 domains and 18 tasks (Sec. 7, p. 6).
- The text says cross-domain transfer helps HC clearly (Fig. 2b), but it doesn't claim a significant jump-start gain for HC (Fig. 2d, p. 6); the figure has no HC bar, so this can't be checked.
- Prop. 4's bound O(sum_g 1/|G(g)|) grows with the number of groups, so "more stable as groups grow" is the authors' framing (Sec. 6, p. 5), not what the bound shows.
- Fig. 2a-c show learning curves for only three domains (DCP, HC, CP); against PG-ELLA the jump-start bars in Fig. 2d are about equal in SM and Bike (Fig. 2d, p. 6).
- Open question (reader's): does group membership have to be given? The paper's "autonomous" refers to learning mappings, not discovering groups.
- Open question (reader's, speculation, not in the paper): a domain here has its own state/action size, whereas a shmup domain (a game) would share the pixel input and a gamepad action space with other games. Then Psi^(g) would not be needed to bridge dimensions, and only the shared L and per-game s(t) remain; that is the same structure as a shared low-rank basis with per-game coefficients.
- Open question (reader's, speculation): carrying this to a pretrained transformer could mean treating a layer's weight delta per game as theta(t), writing it as shared low-rank factors (like LoRA matrices) times a small per-game coefficient vector, and updating the shared factors with an online loss like Eq. 5 while new games arrive. Eq. 5's Hessian weighting would need a tractable stand-in (for example a diagonal Fisher), and sparse s(t) would make each game pick a few basis directions. The paper tests none of this.
- Open question (reader's, speculation): s(t) is a task embedding learned from a policy-gradient step; for a policy that never reads game RAM, s(t) could be inferred from pixels or a few trajectories, but nothing here does that.
